Plant Biosynthesis for Music Production and Biological Data Analysis

An exploration of the translation of plant bioelectricity to sounds for audio engineering.

2026 • PI - Derek Linzy • Department of Music, Spelman College • Spelman Data Science Summer Program

Overview

This interdisciplinary project explores how plant bioelectrical activity can be integrated into music production and biological data analysis. By using electrical signals generated by plants as a real-time data source for sound synthesis, the project investigates how biological systems can drive creative audio processes while remaining grounded in data science and signal processing.

Research Questions

  • How can plant bioelectrical activity be mapped to meaningful musical parameters?
  • What patterns emerge in plant-generated signal data when plants are exposed to environmental changes?
  • Can plant-driven music become a viable form of generative media or commercial creative content?

Creative Innovation

The goals of this project are as follows:

  • To investigate how plant bioelectrical signals can be transformed into structured musical data.
  • To investigate how plant bioelectrical signals can be analyzed using data science techniques.
  • To demonstrating connections between biological signaling and professional music production workflows.

Technical Execution

Utilizing the PlantWave website, I was able to construct a rudimentary chart displaying how each bioelectrical signial deterected by the PlantWave device's probes was mapped to a different pitch, resulting in music "score" by the plant. During this project, we tested the variables pressure, leaf density, and PlantWave sensor probe location (leaves verses roots) to determine how these factors impacted the sound generated by this device. With assistance from our mentor, our group was able to create a small snippet of music generated by a plant's bioelectricity.
