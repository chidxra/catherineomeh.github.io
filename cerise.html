CERISE: Chemical Education Realized through Interactive Storytelling Experiences

A series video game aimed at increasing the engagement of college-aged Black Women within chemistry education.

2026 • PI - Daniel Ashley, Ph.D • Dash Lab Games • Department of Chemistry & Biochemistry, Spelman College

Overview

CERISE is currently being developed within the Unity engine as a 2D pixel-style RPG. The gameplay of CERISE demonstrates the ubiquity and applicability of chemistry in the life of a young Black woman named Abby who is just starting her scientific career. Students can guide Abby through various modules with their own self-contained narratives that incorporate topics and skills typically covered in standard general chemistry curricula.

Research Questions

  • What does representation in video games look like outside of character design?
  • How can does designing a character-driven narrative impact learning verses a plot-driven narrative?
  • How can students across disciplines (ex: chemistry and computer science) collaberate to develop work that creates positive change within education?

Creative Innovation

The goals of this project are as follows:

  • Teach chemistry concepts in a way that involves not just an intellectual commitment, but an emotional one.
  • To show students that not only is chemistry an important part of everyday life, but specifically that it is an important part of their lives.
  • To demonstrate explicitly and implicitly that young Black women will be, and already are, vital members of the STEM community.
  • To design a memorable experience that students actively want to engage with.

Technical Execution

Game Development

This game is modeled after "choose-your-own-adventure" narratives, where the choices players make will allow them to experience a unique version of the game. Some choices result in small differences in dialogue, while larger choices, specially those included in chemistry mini-games, will lead to the success or failure of the chemistry experiment being performed in each module.

Software Utilized

  • Twine - Game writing and narrative branching visualization
  • Gimp - Character sprite sheet design
  • Unity - 2D Game Environment
  • C++ - Character movement programming
